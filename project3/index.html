Design rationale

The main chart uses position along shared time and temperature axes because the central task is comparing trajectories and identifying threshold-crossing years. Historical values are shown as one neutral line, while the four SSP pathways use an ordered cool-to-hot palette. I considered small multiples, but overlaying the scenarios makes their divergence after 2015 immediately visible and allows a single threshold line to support direct comparison.

The location menu and scenario checkboxes are dynamic-query filters that reduce clutter without hiding the available analytical dimensions. The warming-threshold slider turns an abstract temperature curve into a concrete question—when does this pathway first cross 1.5°C, 2°C, or a higher local threshold? The horizon slider supports near-, mid-, and late-century exploration. Hovering provides exact values, while clicking a scenario line changes the linked comparison pathway.

The ranked comparison chart uses horizontal position for anomaly and a common baseline across locations. Its five-year average reduces single-year variability, and clicking a row links the overview back to the detailed chart. I avoided a map because nine points would emphasize geography without showing projected magnitude as effectively; the ranked view better answers where warming is greatest under a selected pathway and time horizon.

Data transformations

Monthly near-surface air temperature (tas) was extracted from the NOAA/Google public CMIP6 Zarr catalog for NCAR CESM2 ensemble member r4i1p1f1. Monthly values were weighted by days per month and aggregated to annual means at the nearest model grid cell for nine locations. Each series is expressed as an anomaly from that location's 1981–2010 historical mean. The comparison chart averages the five years ending at the selected horizon.

Development process

This is an individual portfolio implementation. Development took approximately 12 people-hours across data access, transformation, interaction design, implementation, and testing. The most time-consuming work was extracting a compact reproducible subset from cloud-hosted CMIP6 Zarr stores and coordinating linked interactions so every control updates the chart, summaries, annotations, and comparison view consistently.

Source: NOAA Public CMIP6 Dataset on Google Cloud . This explorer shows one climate model and one ensemble member, not a multi-model uncertainty range; results are projections rather than predictions.
